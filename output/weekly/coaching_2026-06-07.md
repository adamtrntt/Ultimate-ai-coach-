## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect your form score before the midweek quality session.**
You're heading into next week with Form at approximately **-17.7** (CTL 25.9, ATL 43.6). ATL will decay toward ~38 by Monday with full rest Sunday, but you're still carrying meaningful fatigue. On Monday's session, if HR is running 5–8 bpm higher than usual at a given pace, that's a signal to reduce interval intensity slightly rather than chase the target pace. The fitness gain from a slightly conservative session outweighs the risk of digging deeper into the hole.

**2. Cap your easy run HR at 155 bpm — non-negotiable.**
Your Thursday easy run nailed this (avg 138, max 156). Maintain this discipline. With ATL elevated and sleep scores inconsistent, easy days need to genuinely be easy. If you're creeping above 155 on flat terrain, slow down — even if that means 5:50–6:00/km. The goal is aerobic stimulus without adding to fatigue debt.

**3. Target cadence of 80–82 rpm on all runs.**
Your cadence has sat at 77–79 rpm across every run this block. At your goal marathon pace of 4:58/km, higher cadence (80–84 rpm) reduces ground contact time and injury risk, particularly relevant at 102kg. Set a cadence alert on your Garmin at 80 rpm. Don't force it — just use it as a nudge. Apply this first on the easy Thursday run where there's no pace pressure.

**4. Prioritise 7.5–8 hours of sleep before Wednesday's interval session.**
Your HRV has been inconsistent (22–77 range over the block) and sleep quality scores have frequently been 3–4. The night before your key quality session matters most. HRV of 53–58 on recent days suggests you're recovering but not fully adapted. Log off screens earlier Tuesday evening, and treat pre-session sleep as part of the training prescription — it's where the adaptation from last week's sessions is consolidated.

**5. On the long run: stay in Zone 1–2 for the first 60% of the distance.**
Your 6 Jun long run was well-executed, but the 15 May long run (feel: 1, RPE 5) showed what happens when pace drifts into Zone 2+ too early. As distance increases week on week, protect the early kilometres. Keep HR under 160 bpm for the first 10km of any long run. Your body is still adapting to the increased volume — patience in the first half is what makes the second half productive training.
