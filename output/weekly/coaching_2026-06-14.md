## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect easy run HR — hard ceiling of 152 bpm**
Your Monday easy run averaged 143 bpm and hit 162 bpm at peak. At 102 kg in Canberra winter mornings (data shows temps 17–29°C across sessions — watch for warm afternoon slots), easy truly means easy. Keep HR under 152 bpm (your Zone 1/2 boundary) for every minute of every easy run. If a hill pushes you over, walk the hill. Your ATL is at 50 and form is −21 — easy days need to be recovery, not "moderate." Slow down another 10–15 seconds per km if needed.

**2. On the tempo session — trust the prescribed pace, don't chase HR**
Last Wednesday's tempo hit 176 bpm max (97.8% LTHR). For 2 km repeats at 4:25/km, you should be working hard but not touching the ceiling — target max HR of ~170–173 bpm (LTHR−7 to LTHR−4) during the intervals. If you're going out at 4:10–4:15/km to "feel the effort," pull back. The target pace on the plan is 4:25/km — run that pace and let the HR settle where it settles. Racing your tempo intervals three weeks into a build is the fastest way to dig yourself into a hole.

**3. Prioritise sleep this week — aim for 8 hours minimum**
Your HRV hit 41–44 on back-to-back mornings (June 11 and June 14) and sleep scores dropped to 60 and 71 respectively. You're carrying real fatigue. Sleep is the single highest-leverage recovery tool you have. Target lights out by 9:30 pm on nights before key sessions (tempo, long run). Even one additional hour per night across the week will meaningfully shift HRV and lower your ATL heading into the weekend.

**4. Long run — execute the structure, resist pace creep in the fast segments**
Last Saturday's long run averaged 5:16/km overall with the structured pacing (5:20/5:00 segments). Your sub-3:30 goal race pace is 4:58/km. The 8 km @ 5:00 block is close to race pace — treat it as goal-pace familiarisation, not a tempo effort. Keep cadence consistent (you averaged 79.7 rpm last week — nudge toward 80–82 rpm on the faster segments to reduce ground contact time without increasing perceived effort). If HR climbs above 165 bpm during the 5:00/km block, ease to 5:05–5:10/km.

**5. Cadence — build the habit on easy runs**
Across all your easy and long runs, cadence is sitting at 77–80 rpm. For a 102 kg runner targeting sub-3:30, increasing cadence to 80–83 rpm on easy runs reduces vertical oscillation and protects joints at higher mileage. Pick a specific easy run next week and run the final 10 minutes focusing on a quicker, lighter turnover. Don't force it on quality sessions — build the pattern in low-stakes running first.

## 4. One Metric to Monitor

### **Morning HRV**

Your HRV dropped to **41** the morning after the tempo run (June 11) and is sitting at **44** the morning after your biggest long run of the block (June 14). These are the two lowest readings in the 14-day window, and they're landing on the back-to-back hard days — exactly when you need recovery to stick.

Check it first thing, same conditions, every morning. It's the most actionable single number in your dashboard right now.
