## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Wednesday tempo: trust the HR ceiling, not the pace target**
The 2km repeats at 4:25/km are roughly 95% of threshold pace. With your LTHR at 180 and current fatigue (ATL 41.9, form -16.1), HR will spike faster than normal on these intervals. **Cap HR at 175 bpm during the hard efforts** — if you hit that ceiling before the pace does, back off 3–5 sec/km rather than push through. Your interval session on Jun 3 hit 173 bpm on 1km repeats; these 2km efforts at similar intensity will accumulate more fatigue. Running through a suppressed HRV (51 on Jun 8) means the signal is to execute with precision, not heroics.

**2. Saturday long run: treat the middle 5km block (@ 5:20) as the anchor, not the 8km @ 5:00**
The structure is 5km easy → 5km @ 5:20 → 8km @ 5:00 → 1km easy. For a sub-3:30 goal (target MP ~4:58/km), the 8km @ 5:00 is *very close* to marathon pace — at 19km total, that's a significant chunk of race-pace work this early in the block. **Start the 5:20 block conservatively (first 2km closer to 5:25–5:30)** and let your HR tell you if the body is absorbing the load. If avg HR on that segment climbs above 160, the 5:00 block should be treated as 5:05–5:10. Protect the long run's primary purpose: aerobic stimulus, not threshold work.

**3. Push cadence toward 82–84 spm on easy runs**
Your easy run cadence is consistently **77–79 spm** — low for your goal pace and body weight. At 102kg, higher cadence reduces peak ground reaction force per step, which matters for joint load over high-mileage weeks. On Thursday's easy 5.5km, focus on a quick, light foot strike. Set a metronome cue or use a Garmin alert at 82 spm. You don't need to force it on the tempo or long run yet — just the easy day.

**4. Prioritise sleep the night before the Saturday long run (Friday night)**
Sleep on the night of/before your Jun 6 long run was only **6h 22min with a score of 67** — your worst in the window. Your best recent sleep (Jun 1: 9h 5min, score 91) coincided with your best HRV reading (61). Friday night sleep directly governs Saturday execution quality. Aim for **≥7.5 hours**. If you have any late commitments Friday, move them or cut them short. This is a race-day dress rehearsal in terms of preparation.

**5. Wednesday is the load pivot for the week — protect Tuesday and Thursday**
With Wednesday's tempo session being the week's quality anchor and Saturday's long run being the volume anchor, **Tuesday and Thursday are your recovery insurance**. Don't let optional activity (extra walking, added strength volume) accumulate on those days. Your walks added 10–14 load units each in this window — fine contextually, but keep Tuesday especially low. The Wednesday session will be compromised if Tuesday's overall load is high.
