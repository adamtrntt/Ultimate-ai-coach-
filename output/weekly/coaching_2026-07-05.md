## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect the first 48 hours — this is genuine recovery time**
ATL was at 56.6 on Saturday. Even decaying normally, you're still carrying a Form score of around -15 heading into Monday. The easy runs this week are not junk miles — they're a deliberate recalibration. Run the easy sessions genuinely easy: HR cap of **145 bpm**, no exceptions. If HR trends above that in the first 10 minutes, slow down, walk the hills, and accept a slower average pace. At 102 kg in Canberra winter, a 5:40–6:00/km easy pace is completely appropriate.

**2. Cadence target for quality sessions: 83+ spm**
Your cadence across sessions has sat at 79–80 spm on easy runs and 82–83 spm on harder efforts. For sub-3:30 marathon pace (~4:58/km), a cadence of **83–85 spm** is optimal for efficiency and reducing ground contact time. On interval and tempo reps this week, consciously aim for 83+ spm. Set a cadence alert on the Forerunner 165 if the watch supports it. The data shows you can hold 83 under load — now make it automatic.

**3. Interval pacing discipline — hit the target, not under it**
On Monday's 1km repeats you ran ~4:12/km against a 4:20 target. On Wednesday's tempo you were pushing toward 4:30–4:39/km in intervals. This pattern of running faster than prescribed is accumulating extra load without proportional aerobic benefit at this stage of the block. The purpose of prescribed pacing is to target specific physiological zones — the 4:20 target is not a minimum. **Hit the number, not under it.** This becomes more important as weekly load increases toward peak.

**4. Sleep is the free adaptation window — target 7.5+ hours**
Two nights in the past 14 days recorded sleep scores below 65 (2 July: 52, 26 June: 61). Both correlated with the lowest HRV readings of the fortnight (43 and 47 respectively). With CTL needing to climb from 34 to ~55+ over the next 5 weeks, recovery quality directly governs how much fitness you absorb from each session. Prioritise sleep onset before 10pm on nights before quality sessions. The data already shows your best HRV days (61–63 on 24 June and 4 July) align with 7.5h+ sleep — replicate those conditions.

**5. Heat awareness for afternoon sessions**
Several runs in this window recorded max temps of 27–30°C despite Canberra's July winter — likely afternoon runs hitting 16:30–17:30 warmth. The Thursday easy run (2 July, 16:39 start) hit 30°C max. This elevates HR by an estimated 5–8 bpm at a given pace, meaning your perceived zone may be correct even if your HR reads higher than expected. If running afternoon sessions this week, add ~5–7 seconds/km to your planned easy pace to keep HR below 145 bpm. Morning runs (as on the long run and intervals) are preferable for quality sessions.
