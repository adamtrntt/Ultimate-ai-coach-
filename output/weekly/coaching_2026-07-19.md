**1. Today's HRV of 36 is a red flag — protect Monday**
Your HRV has crashed to 36 (lowest in 14 days) with resting HR elevated to 52. If Monday's Runna session is a quality session, consider whether your body can actually absorb it. At minimum, run the warm-up and make a real-time decision based on how you feel at the 10-minute mark. Do not force quality when your autonomic system is waving a white flag. If it's an easy day, keep it truly easy — HR cap of **135 bpm maximum**.

**2. Easy runs must stay genuinely easy — HR cap 135 bpm**
Thursday's easy run averaged 143 bpm, which crept into the lower end of Zone 2. For a 102kg athlete building toward a marathon, protecting easy days matters more than hitting a distance target. In Canberra's winter conditions (sessions showing 6–16°C at start), there's no heat excuse for HR creep. If HR drifts above 135 on an easy day, slow down regardless of pace. Your current easy pace should be ~5:45–6:00/km to keep HR honest.

**3. Tempo and interval sessions: trust the pace, don't chase HR**
Your tempo and interval work is being executed well — 400m reps at 3:45/km (inside 3:55 target) and tempo at 4:20–4:22/km (inside 4:25 target). With race pace at 4:58/km, keep these sessions anchored to the prescribed paces rather than chasing a HR number. Max HR of 165–168 during quality work (still 12–15 bpm below LTHR of 180) confirms you have appropriate headroom.

**4. Cadence: consistent opportunity for improvement**
Cadence across all runs sits at **79–83 rpm** — this is on the lower end for efficient marathon running (optimal typically 85–90 rpm). At 102kg, higher cadence reduces ground contact time and impact loading per stride. During your easy and long runs next week, try adding 2–3 rpm by shortening stride slightly. Don't force it during quality sessions, but on easy days, set a target of **82–83 rpm** as a floor and work toward it.

**5. Sleep is a training variable — target 8+ hours this week**
Sleep quality has been consistently rated 2–4 (poor to fair) across the past 14 days, with scores dipping to 52–67 on the hardest training days. With HRV already suppressed post-long-run, the fastest route to absorbing this week's load is prioritising sleep over everything else. Target being in bed by 9:30pm on nights before quality sessions. The data is clear: your best HRV readings (61–62) align with your longer sleep nights (9.3–9.6 hours on Jul 7–8).
