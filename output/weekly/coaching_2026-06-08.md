**1. Cap easy run HR at 155 bpm — and accept the pace consequence**

**2. Wednesday tempo: target the marathon pace segments at 4:55–5:05/km, not faster**

The plan calls for 2 x 2km at 4:25/km. Your May 27 Over and Unders session showed a max HR of 176 (89% of max) during threshold work — that's pushing toward zone 4 at tempo efforts. The sub-3:30 goal pace is **4:58/km**. The 4:25/km reps are race-pace-minus efforts designed to build lactate tolerance. If HR is blowing through 175+ on the first rep, back off 5 seconds/km — the cumulative fatigue from the last two weeks means your lactate threshold HR may be slightly depressed this week.

**3. Saturday long run — protect the easy 5km opener religiously**

The planned 19km has a 5km easy opener before stepping into 5km at 5:20/km and 8km at 5:00/km. Your long run data from the past two weeks shows you've been good at starting conservative (120 bpm opener on the 17km run). Replicate that. Don't drift above 150 bpm in the first 5km regardless of how fresh you feel. The 8km at 5:00/km in the back half is where this run builds your marathon fitness — you need to get there with something in the tank.

Your June 6 long run followed a night of only 6h22m sleep with a quality score of 67. You then recorded your lowest HRV (46) and the run, while executed well, produced a feel score of 1/5 (low). For the 19km this Saturday — your longest run of the block — you need 7.5–8h sleep on both Thursday and Friday. This is not optional: the progressive long run requires glycogen restoration and neuromuscular readiness that only sleep delivers. If Thursday's easy run is late in the day and it's cutting into sleep, move it to the morning.

**5. Raise cadence toward 80–82 spm on all runs**

Across every run in the past 14 days, cadence has sat at **77–81 spm** — mostly at the low end (77–79 on easy runs). At your weight, a low cadence increases ground contact time and vertical oscillation, which raises injury risk as long run distances climb toward 20km+. On the Saturday long run, use the opening 5km easy section to lock in 80 spm before the pace work begins. Even a 1–2 spm improvement reduces impact load meaningfully over a 19km effort.
