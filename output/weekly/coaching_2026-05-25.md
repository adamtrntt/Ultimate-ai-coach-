## 2. Risk Assessment

### 🔴 Rapid CTL Decline — Detraining Risk

CTL fell from 32.2 to 20.0 in 23 days (−37.8%). This is substantial. When training resumes aggressively after detraining, connective tissue lags behind cardiovascular fitness recovery — tendons and bones don't rebound in 1–2 weeks the way aerobic capacity does. **The athlete is now in the zone where overuse injury risk is elevated** because they may feel capable of more than their tissues can safely absorb.

### 🔴 Long Run Intensity — HR Too High

The May 23 long run averaged 161 bpm — the exact top of Z2 (threshold for this athlete: 161 bpm = Z2/Z3 boundary). For a long run, **the target should be Z1–low Z2, or 148–158 bpm**. Running at or above Z2 ceiling for 72-minute efforts defeats the purpose of aerobic base development and adds meaningful stress on top of a recovering CTL. If this continues weekly, accumulated fatigue will limit quality on harder sessions.

### 🟡 Sleep Quality — Persistently Poor

Sleep quality scores during travel: multiple nights rated **4/5 (poor)**. Sleep scores 16–62 on several nights (May 1, 4, 12, 14, 15). Even post-return, sleep quality is mostly rated 2/5 ("fair") with occasional 3/5. **No nights rated 1/5 (excellent) since May 13.** For recovery and adaptation, this is a limiting factor. Sleep duration is mostly adequate (7–9 hours), but quality is the issue.

### 🟡 Form Turning Negative During Low CTL Base

The athlete is now at ATL 24.9 vs CTL 21.8 (form = −3.1). This is a mild negative, but the concern is the **absolute CTL level is low (~22)**. Negative form at CTL 50+ is normal training stress; negative form at CTL 22 means the recent sessions are disproportionately large relative to the fitness base. The ramp rate has jumped from −4.6 to +1.3 in one week — a rapid reversal that needs a controlled continuation, not further acceleration.

### 🟡 Complete Swim Absence

If transitioning to a marathon block, swim absence is fine. If still nominally in a triathlon block, **zero swim sessions in 28 days** represents a discipline-specific detraining concern. This needs a clear decision: commit to marathon transition now, or maintain swim frequency at ≥2x/week until the block officially switches.
