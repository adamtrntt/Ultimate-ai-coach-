## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

### 1. Wednesday Tempo — Own the Discomfort, Not the Pace
For the 5km tempo, target **HR 170–176 bpm** (Z3/low Z4) rather than chasing a specific pace. At current fitness, honest tempo effort at 102kg in Canberra winter should land around **5:00–5:15/km**. Do not start at goal marathon pace (4:58/km) — you're not there yet aerobically. A negative split structure (first 2km controlled, last 2km pushing) will give you better training data than blowing up. If HR exceeds 180 bpm (LTHR), ease off — you're in the wrong zone for a tempo stimulus.

### 2. Saturday Long Run — Hard Cap at HR 163 bpm
Last week's 13km averaged 161 bpm and spent the bulk of time in Z3. For the 15km progressive run, enforce a **hard ceiling of 163 bpm** for the first 10km, then allow effort to build naturally in the final 5km. This may mean running 5:45–6:00/km in the early stages, especially on hills. That's correct. A true progressive long run builds aerobic efficiency; last week's effort was more of a moderate-hard run throughout. Your long run HR average should land closer to **155–158 bpm**, not 161+.

### 3. Cadence — Target 80 rpm on Every Run
You're consistently at 77–78 rpm. Add a simple cue: **aim for 80 steps per minute (each foot)**. On easy runs, this is where to start. Don't force it on tempo or intervals — let it develop naturally. The Garmin 165 shows cadence in real-time. Check it every 5 minutes on Thursday's easy run and gently increase turnover if below 80. Even +1–2 rpm reduces stride length slightly and distributes load more evenly — important at your body weight over marathon distances.

### 4. Sleep is Training — Prioritise Quality This Week
HRV dipped to 42 post-long-run Saturday, recovered to 60 by Monday — good adaptation response. But multiple nights of sleep quality score 2–4 over the last month tell a consistent story: **you're not recovering as well as the hours suggest.** Before the Wednesday tempo, prioritise 7.5+ hours with a consistent wake time. If HRV is below 50 on Wednesday morning, treat the tempo as a controlled aerobic run rather than a hard effort. Don't push quality sessions on compromised recovery — at 13 weeks out, you have time to be patient.

### 5. Manage the Load Spike Risk
Your ATL (24.9) is already above CTL (21.8), meaning you're in a mild fatigue state starting this week. Adding a tempo, easy run, and a 15km long run is the right plan — but **do not add volume beyond Runna's prescription**. Resist the urge to tack on extra kilometres because sessions feel good. The long run jump from 13km to 15km is a ~15% increase in your key session — that's at the upper end of appropriate. If Saturday's conditions are hot or you're carrying fatigue from Wednesday/Thursday, cap at 14km and come back strong the following week.
