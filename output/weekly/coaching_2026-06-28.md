## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect easy run HR — strict Z1 ceiling of 152 bpm**
Your easy runs (Mon, Thu) are landing at avg HR 134–138, which is good, but you're occasionally touching 152–154 bpm on easy efforts (visible in this week's data). With CTL building and a quality session mid-week, those easy days need to stay genuinely easy. If HR nudges toward 148+ on flat terrain, back off the pace — even if that means slowing to 6:00+/km. At 102 kg in winter Canberra conditions, don't let the cool air tempt you into running faster than your aerobic system needs.

**2. Cadence focus on easy runs — target 83–85 spm**
Your cadence is sitting at 79–82 spm across most sessions. For a 102 kg runner targeting sub-3:30, increasing cadence slightly reduces ground contact time and impact load on joints. On your Monday easy run next week, consciously try to hold 83–85 spm for the first 3 km and note how it feels. Don't force it — just experiment. Shorter, quicker steps at the same HR is a useful efficiency cue.

**3. On the quality/workout session — trust the plan paces, don't chase the effort**
Your progressive and interval sessions are trending slightly hot on intensity (peak HR 170, intensity factor ~100% on the progressive run). With your LTHR at 180, you have headroom — but hitting near-threshold HR on mid-week quality days in week 4 of a 9-week block is a pattern that can compound fatigue. If Runna prescribes threshold-type work next week, focus on hitting the prescribed paces (likely around 4:50–5:05/km for marathon-pace work) rather than chasing effort. Your best 5 km pace data (4:35–4:44/km) shows the engine is there — now it's about precision, not proving it.

**4. Long run — enforce conversational pace, target avg HR under 145 bpm**
Your 12 km long run averaged 143 bpm — right at the target ceiling. If distance increases next week (which it likely will), don't let average HR creep above 145 bpm, and if it does, slow down rather than push through. Your half marathon at 147 bpm for 21 km is a good reference point — long runs should feel noticeably easier than that. The goal is time on feet and aerobic development, not fitness testing.

**5. Prioritise sleep — aim for 7.5–8 hrs minimum**
HRV averaged only 53 over the past 14 days, and sleep has been your most variable wellness marker (scores 61–91, two nights under 6.5 hours recorded). With your ramp rate sitting above 2.0 CTL/week, sleep is now your primary recovery lever. A 6.4-hour night (26 Jun, score 61) following a hard training week is a pattern worth breaking. Set a consistent bedtime this week. The CTL you're building is only useful if you can absorb it.
