## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect easy run HR — cap at 145 bpm, not pace**
Thursday's 8km easy averaged 143 bpm and hit 155 bpm — that's technically still Zone 1 but at the ceiling. In Canberra's afternoon heat (sessions hitting 29–30°C), pace-based easy running will push you aerobically harder than intended. Next week, ignore pace on easy days entirely. If it's warm, run by HR: **cap at 145 bpm**. Expect pace to drop to 3:30–3:45/km or slower. That's correct. The aerobic stimulus is the same; the recovery cost is lower.

**2. Cadence target: 82 spm minimum on all runs**
You're sitting at 79–80 spm on easy runs. For a runner at 102kg targeting sub-3:30, higher cadence reduces impact forces and injury risk at the volume you're building. This week, focus on hitting **82 spm as a floor** on easy runs, and **84–85 spm on tempo work**. Don't force it — use your watch's cadence alert if available, and think about "quicker feet" rather than bigger strides. The tempo sessions (83–84 spm) show you can hit this; replicate it on easier days.

**3. Tempo work — don't drift above 170 bpm**
The on/off km session hit a max of 171 bpm with average tempo intervals at ~158 bpm. That's in Zone 3, which is appropriate for marathon-specific tempo. However, your LTHR is 180 — if next week's tempo session pushes max HR above 175 bpm, you're losing specificity and accumulating unnecessary fatigue. **Target tempo intervals at 155–165 bpm.** If you're running 4:25–4:30/km and HR is staying in that band, you're dialed in perfectly.

**4. Sleep is a priority — target 8+ hours before the long run**
The HRV of 24 and sleep score of 38 on 25 July directly preceded today's long run. Despite it going well, that's a risk you don't want to repeat at higher intensity sessions. The next 3 weeks are your last real build opportunities before taper — recovery quality determines how much adaptation you get from the load. **Target 8 hours in bed the night before any quality session (tempo, intervals, long run).** This is free fitness.

**5. On the long run — introduce a marathon-pace segment**
Your long run aerobic control is outstanding — 30km at HR 131 bpm is a genuine strength. But with 5 weeks to race day, it's time to start adding specificity. If next week's long run has a middle or closing segment, ask yourself whether you can include 3–5km at **4:58–5:05/km** (goal marathon pace) while HR stays below 160 bpm. This teaches your body the specific metabolic demand of race pace under fatigue. Don't force it if the Runna plan doesn't prescribe it — but be mentally ready to use part of the run for race-pace rehearsal if the session structure allows.

## 4. One Metric to Monitor

### **HRV — specifically the morning reading before quality sessions**

Your HRV dropped to 24 on 25 July (vs a 14-day range of 36–60), coinciding with poor sleep (5h43m, score 38) and elevated resting HR (53 bpm vs baseline 47–49). This is the single most actionable early-warning signal you have.

With 5 weeks to race day, the training you're about to do in the next 2–3 weeks represents your final fitness gains before taper. Sessions completed on a suppressed HRV with poor sleep return a fraction of the adaptation of well-recovered sessions — and carry disproportionate injury risk.

**What to do with it:** If you wake up before a tempo or interval session with HRV below 40 and resting HR above 52 bpm, treat that as a yellow flag. Downgrade the session to an easy run, or shift it 24 hours. The long run is more forgiving on low-HRV days (easy pace self-corrects). Quality sessions are not.

You already have the data coming in daily — use it as a go/no-go check before every hard session this week.
