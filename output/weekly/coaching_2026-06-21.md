## 3. Coaching Notes for Next Week

**1. Protect easy days — HR cap 150 bpm on all non-quality runs**
The Jun 13 long run drifted to avg 155 bpm, and even the Jun 18 "easy" 7km hit 153 bpm max. Your current CTL (~30) means your aerobic system is still developing. For any easy or recovery run next week, treat **150 bpm as a hard ceiling** — walk if needed on hills. The Form is sitting at −16 to −17; easy runs need to actually be easy to allow CTL to compound without digging the ATL hole deeper.

**2. On the 800m reps: trust the pace, watch the HR ceiling**
Last week's 800s hit **max HR 173 bpm (96% LTHR)**. That's very close to LTHR for an interval session that still has significant volume. For next week's 800m session, aim for your best reps at or around **4:10–4:15/km** as before, but if HR is exceeding **175 bpm** mid-rep, extend recovery to 2 minutes. The goal is quality of effort across all reps, not a personal best on rep 1 at the expense of reps 5 and 6.

**3. On the tempo 2km reps: anchor to pace, not feel**
The Jun 15 tempo hit max HR 174 bpm — that's 97% LTHR, which is running into threshold/threshold+ territory. Target pace is **~4:25/km** for the 2km reps. If you're hitting that pace and HR is climbing above **172–173 bpm**, back off 3–4 sec/km. You want to accumulate time at tempo, not blow up early and make the second rep a survival exercise. Last week's session RPE was logged at 6/10, which is appropriate — try to keep it there.

**4. Sleep is the free performance variable — prioritise 8h before quality sessions**
In the last 14 days, your three worst sleep nights (**6.4h–7.0h**) aligned with your hardest training days. HRV dropped to 41 after one of those sequences. Before your interval and tempo sessions next week, aim for **≥7.5h with a target of 8h**. If HRV is ≤45 on a key session morning, treat that session as a controlled effort rather than pushing for new pacing benchmarks — the physiological signal is telling you adaptation isn't complete.

**5. Canberra winter heat is still a factor — manage warm-up execution**
Session temperatures have ranged from 13–31°C across the 14-day window, with some runs starting cool but finishing in heat (Jun 15: 17→30°C; Jun 18: 16→31°C). Your HR on easy runs may be inflating in the back half due to thermal load. If afternoon sessions exceed 25°C, either shift to morning or treat the final 2km as pure cool-down regardless of planned pace. Don't let heat-driven HR drift compromise your session assessment.
